BASIC TRAINING

Essential Knowledge for New Recruits

1. Electronics Basics

Electricity is invisible, but it behaves exactly like water flowing through a pipe. To build circuits, you only need to understand three main concepts:

  • The Three Pillars of Electronics:
  • Voltage (V):
    Think of this as Water Pressure. It's the "push" that makes electricity move. A 9V battery pushes harder than a 1.5V battery.
  • Current (I):
    Think of this as Water Flow. It's the actual amount of electrons moving through the wire. We measure it in Amps.
  • Resistance (R):
    Think of this as Pipe Width. A resistor creates a "squeeze" in the pipe, slowing down the flow (Current). We measure it in Ohms (Ω).
  • The Rule (Ohm's Law):
    Voltage = Current x Resistance. If you increase Resistance (thinner pipe), Current goes down (less flow).

2. The Breadboard

The Breadboard is your construction base. It lets you connect wires without soldering. The holes are connected underneath in a specific pattern.

  • How Connections Work:
  • Power Rails (The Long Lines):
    On the top and bottom (Red and Blue lines), all holes are connected Horizontally. We use these for 5V (Red) and GND (Blue) so power is available everywhere.
  • Terminal Strips (The Middle Area):
    In the middle, holes are connected in Vertical Columns of 5 holes (e.g., column 1a-1e is connected).
  • The Divider (Middle Gap):
    The gap in the center separates the left side from the right side. This allows us to plug in chips (ICs) without shorting their pins together.

3. Installing the Software

The "Arduino IDE" is the tool we use to write code and upload it to the board. Follow these steps to get your computer ready.

  1. Download: Go to arduino.cc/software and download the IDE for your OS (Windows, Mac, or Linux).
  2. Select Board: Open the IDE. Go to Tools > Board > Arduino AVR Boards > Arduino Uno.
  3. Select Port: Plug in your Arduino. Go to Tools > Port and select the one that appears (e.g., COM3 or /dev/cu.usbmodem...).
  4. Test Upload: Go to File > Examples > 01.Basics > Blink. Click the arrow button (Upload). If the board's built-in light blinks, you are ready!
